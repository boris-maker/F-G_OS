Fundación Federico Jorge Klemm is pleased to announce the beginning of its pro tempore custody of Un meteorito para la Sociedad Científica Argentina del año 2105, a work by the artist duo Faivovich & Goldberg whose horizon lies at the dawn of the twenty-second century.

When the Sociedad Científica Argentina (SCA) once again decides the fate of the meteorite on July 9, 2105, this act will stand among its antecedents. On October 1, 2026, in a private ceremony held in one of the Fundación’s galleries before a limited number of witnesses, the first institutional custody agreement for the work was signed.

Speaking at the ceremony were Cintia Mezza, Manager of Collection, Archive and Public Programs at the Fundación; Sergio Baur, President of the Academia Nacional de Bellas Artes; and José Sellés-Martínez, First Vice President of the SCA. The work enters the Klemm Collection as the first loan ever registered in its inventory, cared for alongside the Fundación’s own holdings until May 25, 2032.

Following the signing, the capsule was hoisted into place for the occasion. A steel ring embraces the gallery’s central column. From it extends the Escala Pro Tempore, a three-meter iron bar that segments the time of the work, and from its end hangs the piece.

The work holds a 41-kilogram iron meteorite, received by the artists in 2016 from an anonymous benefactor, on whose behalf they sought to donate it to the SCA. The donation could not be completed. Faced with that impossibility, Faivovich & Goldberg turned it into a time capsule: the meteorite was encapsulated on May 25, 2018, and the decision on its fate deferred to July 9, 2105. On that date, the SCA may once again choose to incorporate into its holdings the meteorite in the belly of the sculpture.

Conceived from the outset as durational work, the piece poses a problem of intergenerational transmission: how to sustain, over eighty-seven years, the material, legal, symbolic, and economic legibility of a will addressed to future generations. The work moves through time in a sequence of pro tempore custodies. After a foundational custody held by the artists from 2018 to 2025, whose conclusion was certified through a blockchain record on July 9, 2025, it passes from one custodian to the next every seven years—a septennium—until a final term of three years closes the chain. The Fundación is the first institution to take on the task. Through this system of circulation, the artists articulate mechanisms of governance that safeguard the integrity of the work along its physical and conceptual journey toward the next century, while opening new narratives around acquisition, display, and conservation.

In 1999, Federico Klemm set down in his will the terms of his legacy: the Academia Nacional de Bellas Artes would oversee the Fundación’s administration, and once the fund sustaining the institution was exhausted, the Collection would be bequeathed to the Argentine State. As Cintia Mezza noted during the ceremony, the Fundación does not own the meteorite, which it looks after for a septennium, nor does it regard itself as the owner of the Collection, but as its custodian: in both cases it exercises a pro tempore custody. Both are wills projected beyond those who formulated them, and both rest on an act of trust.

Over the course of its septennium, the Fundación proposes three lines of work. The first is an Annual Colloquium, held every October 1, on heritage management, collection transitions, and institutional temporality. The second is an Association of Friends of the Future, a community devoted to the guiding ideas of Federico Klemm: the prize for young artists, free admission, education, and access to the Archive. The third brings the teenagers of the Fundación’s education program into the Colloquium, through alliances with universities. In this way, those who will one day receive the Collection begin imagining its futures now.

In 2032, the capsule will leave the Fundación for a custodian yet to be named. Seventy-three years will still separate it from its rendezvous.
