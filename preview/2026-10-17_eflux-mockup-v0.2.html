The Fundación Federico Jorge Klemm is pleased to announce the beginning of its custody pro tempore of Un meteorito para la Sociedad Científica Argentina del año 2105, a work by Faivovich & Goldberg whose horizon lies at the start of the twenty-second century.

On October 1, 2026, in a private ceremony with a small group of invited guests in the Foundation's exhibition room in Buenos Aires, the artists and the Foundation signed the first institutional custody agreement for the work, a loan (comodato) for the period 2025–2032, and the piece entered the Foundation's care. The capsule was presented and hoisted, suspended from Escala Pro Tempore, a steel bar of approximately 100 kilograms that materializes the segmentation of the work's time into custody periods, in a first fitting of the complete installation. Words were read by Cintia Mezza, for the Fundación Klemm, who set out three proposals for the coming years: an annual colloquium, an Asociación de Amigos del Futuro, and the Foundation's programme for adolescents and its university alliances. She was followed by Sergio Baur, President of the Academia Nacional de Bellas Artes, and José Sellés Martínez, First Vice-President of the Sociedad Científica Argentina, who acknowledged the trajectory that brought the meteorite to this point. The signed agreement was recorded the same day in the project's archive on the InterPlanetary File System (IPFS), a decentralized storage network.

Begun in 2016, the work starts from the legitimate possession of a 41-kilogram iron meteorite from Campo del Cielo, given to the artists by an anonymous donor, whose incorporation into the holdings of the Sociedad Científica Argentina could not be completed. Faivovich & Goldberg turned an unfinished donation into a time capsule: the meteorite was sealed on May 25, 2018, and the decision on its destination deferred to July 9, 2105, when the Society may once again choose to incorporate into its holdings the meteorite held within the sculpture. Conceived from the outset as a work in time, it poses a problem of intergenerational transmission: how to sustain, over eighty-seven years, the material, legal, symbolic and economic legibility of a will addressed to future generations.

The project's temporal structure is organized around a foundational custody, held by the artists between 2018 and 2025, followed by a sequence of custodies pro tempore: eleven seven-year periods and a final three-year period, assigned to the Society itself. The Fundación Klemm holds the second of these periods and the first entrusted to an institution. Through this system of circulation the artists articulate governance mechanisms to guarantee the integrity of the work, accompanying its physical and conceptual journey to its appointment in the next century.

The piece joins the Klemm Collection as a loan, cared for to the same standards as the Foundation's own holdings; authorship and the conceptual integrity of the work remain with the artists. It is accompanied by two further works by Faivovich & Goldberg: Escala Pro Tempore and Anillo para la Fundación Klemm, a clamp made for a column of the Foundation's building. In 2032 the piece will pass to the next institution in the chain.

Faivovich & Goldberg are Guillermo Faivovich (b. 1977, Buenos Aires) and Nicolás Goldberg (b. 1978, Paris); they live and work in Buenos Aires. For twenty years their work has addressed the meteorites of Campo del Cielo, in the Argentine Chaco.

The Fundación Federico Jorge Klemm was created in 1995 by the collector and patron Federico Klemm. Its collection of more than 760 works is on permanent display, with free admission. By the will of its founder, the collection is itself held in custody: it is to pass to the national State, under the oversight of the Academia Nacional de Bellas Artes.
